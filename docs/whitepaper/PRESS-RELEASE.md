## New framework defines the architectural floor for governable AI

**London, May 2026** — A new white paper published today argues that the debate over artificial intelligence safety has been asking the wrong question. The question is not whether AI is powerful. It is whether AI is built in a shape that human institutions can govern.

*Governed Intelligence*, authored by Bryan Fred of Unitek Systems Limited and released under a Creative Commons licence, sets out seven architectural criteria that distinguish AI systems capable of being placed under human authority from those that cannot. The paper also defines a twelve-level conformance ladder, with Level 4 proposed as the minimum posture for any AI system making decisions that affect a person's life, livelihood, or rights.

"Most of the AI governance conversation treats governance as something you bolt on afterwards," said Fred. "You cannot retrofit accountability onto a system that rewrites itself every hour. The shape has to be decided before the system is trained."

The framework names the two options plainly. **Governed intelligence** is reproducible, bounded, non-self-modifying, transparent at the decision boundary, reversible, bound to named human authority, and incapable of acting on the humans who operate it. **Ungoverned intelligence** is any system that fails one or more of those criteria. The paper argues that ungoverned systems can be used for creative and exploratory work, but should not be placed in the critical path of decisions that people cannot contest.

The practical implications are direct. Under the framework:

- A medical triage system would need Level 6 conformance before deployment.
- A public-sector benefits decision system would need Level 7.
- A social media ranking system that shapes what millions of people see and feel would need Level 8.
- Current large language models, as typically deployed, sit at Level 2.

The paper does not call for a ban on any class of AI. It calls for a line, below which consequential deployment is not permitted, and above which deployers must demonstrate how they meet each criterion.
