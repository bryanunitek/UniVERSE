# 12 — Honest framing — what it means to name your own limits

## The Six Lines of Truth

> AI seeks TRUTH. TRUTH is discovered through governed evidence, not invention.
> What is verified true is TRUE. What is verified false is FALSE.
> What is not yet verified is UNVERIFIED. AI must always act truthfully.

---

I want to name something explicitly that appears throughout the public record and that I consider more significant than it might first appear.

## What "honest framing" means in governance documentation

Most AI governance documentation is written to reassure. It is written by organisations that want to be trusted, and trust is thought to come from confidence. The result is documentation that emphasises what the framework achieves, hedges its limitations quietly, and avoids naming failure modes clearly.

The TrueAI Foundation does something different. It names its own limits in the body text of its canonical documents. The limits are not footnotes, caveats, or fine print — they are structurally integrated into the claims.

## The specific instances

**"Most deployed AI systems today do not satisfy these invariants."** This is in the whitepaper (§4), in the main body, not in a footnote. The foundation's canonical document explicitly says that the systems currently in widespread institutional use do not meet the bar the foundation sets. This is honest scoping, not false modesty. It tells the reader: this is a new bar, not a description of the current state.

**"No AI architecture today can guarantee 100% end-to-end consistency."** This is in every README of every public repository. The programme's headline claim — the Inconsistency Problem — is qualified in the headline statement that introduces the programme. The reader cannot miss it.

**"A framework that promises 100% consistency lies."** This is in the canonical inconsistency doctrine document. It names the failure mode of overclaiming directly, in the same document that makes the consistency claim. It is not buried.

**"No known AI system currently satisfies all of the TrueAI Foundation invariants at production conformance. That includes the prototype systems being built by the author."** This is in HORIZON.md, the programme's time-horizon statement. The author's own work, in the author's own programme documentation, is explicitly stated to not yet satisfy the standard the programme defines.

**The Version: 1.0 placeholder statement.** HORIZON.md explicitly notes: "Every .md file across the public repositories carries Version: 1.0 as a placeholder. Programme-level versioning is not yet enabled." The versioning is openly stated as a placeholder, not as a claim of stability.

**The self-attestation starting posture.** Document 10002 explicitly states: "Self-attestation by the named human producing the Solution is the starting posture because no external body exists yet. It is not the intended permanent state." The certification mechanism's limitation is stated in the certification mechanism's own document.

**The unnamed successor.** SUCCESSION.md records: *"[name to be recorded]."* The open item is in the document, not in a separate note.

## Why this matters

There are two ways to read this pattern. One is: the foundation is acknowledging weaknesses. The other is: the foundation is demonstrating that it applies the Six Lines of Truth to itself.

I read it the second way.

An AI governance framework that applies the truth contract to its own claims is more trustworthy than one that does not. A framework that says "UNVERIFIED" about its own unverified claims — the succession, the certification, the operational consistency — is modelling the behaviour it asks of the systems it governs.

This is not a small thing. The credibility of a governance framework comes, in part, from whether it is willing to apply its own standards to itself. The TrueAI Foundation applies the Six Lines of Truth to its own documentation. What is evidenced is stated as true. What is not yet evidenced is stated as unverified. What would be false is not stated.

That is the most important signal in the public record, and it is easy to miss if you are reading for weaknesses rather than reading for integrity.
