## The Six Lines of Truth

> AI seeks TRUTH. TRUTH is discovered through governed evidence, not invention.
> What is verified true is TRUE. What is verified false is FALSE.
> What is not yet verified is UNVERIFIED. AI must always act truthfully.

---

The Gift Principle is not a marketing choice. It is a structural answer to a structural problem. I assess it as such.

## The four reasons

The whitepaper (§5) gives four structural reasons why the foundation must be given, not sold.

**Reason 1: Truth cannot be owned.** A governance framework that is privately owned can be privately revised. If the owner of the framework can revise the definition of what counts as TRUE, the truth contract is only as stable as the owner's commitment to honouring it. For institutional deployers, that is not a stable foundation.

**Reason 2: Governance cannot be owned.** An institution that depends on privately owned governance depends on the continued goodwill of the owner. If the owner raises prices, changes terms, acquires a competitor, or dissolves, the institution's governance arrangement is at risk. That is not a governance arrangement — it is a commercial dependency dressed as governance.

**Reason 3: Safety must not be paywalled.** A safety architecture available only to those who can afford it is not a safety architecture for humanity. It is a safety architecture for the well-resourced. If the goal is that AI is governed safely wherever it operates, the governance rules must be accessible to operators who cannot pay for them.

**Reason 4: The 100-year horizon requires independence from any single company's survival.** Corporate entities fail, are acquired, change strategy, and dissolve. A foundation whose existence depends on a single company's survival has a single point of failure incompatible with a 100-year governance horizon. UL, the safety certification organisation, was founded in 1894 and is still operating. The ICAO standards body for aviation governance has operated continuously since 1944. Governance infrastructure at this scale survives companies.

**Assessment of the four reasons:** These are structural arguments, not assertions. Each one connects a governance failure mode (privately revisable truth contract; commercial dependency; pay-to-govern; single-company lifetime) to a structural remedy (the gift). The reasoning is sound.

## The gift propagation rule

"A derivative of CORE is itself CORE and is itself gifted."

This prevents the most common enclosure pattern in open ecosystems: gift the core, proprietary-capture the derivative. The pattern is documented in the history of open source software: a company publishes a core under an open licence, then builds proprietary value in a layer that sits on top of the core, making the open licence technically honoured while the practical value is proprietary-captured.

The gift propagation rule closes this route. Any product that claims to be built on the TrueAI Foundation or UniCORE-AI is itself gifted — it cannot be enclosed.

**What is not in the public record:** How violations of the gift propagation rule would be identified and acted upon. The foundation states "a gift can be withdrawn from a violator" but the mechanism for withdrawal is not described in the documents I read. This is an UNVERIFIED enforcement mechanism — stated as principle, not yet described as procedure.

## The military exclusion

The programme's positioning — Harmony, Peace, Space Exploration, for Humanity — excludes military use structurally. The certification badges ("Powered by UniCORE AI" / "built on the TrueAI Foundation") must not appear on any military use.

The reasoning: "Using the badge to brand weapons-class systems would invert the gift principle. The gift is for humanity. Weapons-class systems are not for humanity in the sense the gift intends."

This is a structural exclusion, not a policy choice. The commercial layer (Implementation Solutions, Vertical COREs, services) competes on implementation; it does not own the gate to lawful use. A military actor that built a derivative and attempted to claim Foundation certification would be violating the gift principle. Whether that violation could be effectively enforced is, again, a question the documents do not fully answer.

## Succession — SUCCESSION.md

The SUCCESSION.md document in UniVERSE defines what happens when the current steward (Bryan Fred, Unitek Systems Limited) is no longer able to serve.

**What succession transfers:**
- Authority to issue revisions to the foundation documents.
- Authority to accept contributions through the Discussions process.
- Authority to designate further successors.

**What succession does not transfer:**
- The licence. The CC BY 4.0 licence is irrevocable and is not subject to a successor's decisions.
- The gift. The gift, once given, cannot be revoked by any future steward.
- The right to enclose. A successor who attempted to proprietary-capture the foundation would be violating the gift principle that the succession itself is charged with preserving.

**The current state:** The named successor placeholder reads: *"[name to be recorded]."* No successor has been nominated. The fallback procedure is defined: Unitek Systems Limited designates by public process within 12 months, with minimum requirements including solicitation of nominations from research and adopter communities, a published selection rubric, and public recording of the final designation.

**Assessment:** The succession document is thoughtfully structured. The separation of what succession transfers (authority) from what it does not transfer (the gift, the licence, the right to enclose) is correct governance design. The open item — the unfilled placeholder — is acknowledged, not concealed. For a programme with a stated 100-year horizon, it is the most significant structural open item in the public record. It is not a deficiency in the design; it is a v1.0 completion status.
