**AI has no manual. But the laws already exist. And the liability is coming.**

Three facts define this moment for AI in serious institutional use:

**1. There is no manual.** No agreed operating standard exists for how AI must behave when it sits in the critical path of consequential decisions. Consumer-grade AI was shipped to everyone — law firms, banks, hospitals included — without the grounding and accountability that institutional use actually requires.

**2. But the laws already exist.** The EU AI Act, sectoral regulation, professional-conduct duties, data-protection law — all in force now. The obligations arrived *before* anyone wrote the method for meeting them. Law first. Manual absent.

**3. The liability is coming.** That gap resolves as liability. When ungrounded AI produces a consequential error inside an institutional decision, someone answerable will be held to account. Not *if* — *when*.

So we wrote the missing manual — as a public framework, given freely. A simple grid on two axes: **Consumer ↔ Institutional** (best-effort vs. grounded & accountable) and **AI → Assistant → Development → Paired producer**.

Six levels emerge. And one cell is deliberately empty:

**"Development Consumer AI" cannot exist.**

Once AI crosses into *building the systems* others run on the critical path, the Institutional standard becomes mandatory. Developing such systems to a best-effort, ungrounded, unaccountable standard isn't a cheaper option — it's a non-permitted, liability-generating one. Compliance isn't a feature you bolt on afterward. It's the admission requirement to be development-grade AI at all.

Public, free, CC BY 4.0. No product to buy — an argument about what AI *must be*, written before the lawsuits force one into existence.
